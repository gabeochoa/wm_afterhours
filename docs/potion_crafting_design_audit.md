# Design Audit: Potion Crafting Screen

*Audited against the Apple Human Interface Guidelines (1987 Desktop Interface)*
*Date: February 7, 2026*

---

## Audit Summary: Alchemist's Workshop

### Critical Issues (Fix Immediately)

1. **[Color as Sole Differentiator for Recipes — HIG 3.1, 8.1]**: The recipe list relies entirely on colored circles (red for Healing Potion, blue for Mana Elixir, orange for Fire Bomb, green for Antidote) to visually distinguish potions from each other. Without color, all four recipes appear as identical circles with adjacent text. HIG 3.1 explicitly requires that "color coding is redundant with shape/position/pattern" and the design must "work in black and white first." A colorblind user would see four identical gray circles.
   → **Fix**: Add a secondary visual differentiator to each recipe icon — distinct shapes (heart for healing, droplet for mana, flame for fire bomb, leaf for antidote), unique patterns, or small symbolic glyphs inside the circles. Each potion must be distinguishable without relying on color.

2. **[Cryptic Single-Letter Ingredient Abbreviations — HIG 1.3, 7.1]**: The "Required Ingredients" section beneath the brewing progress uses single-character abbreviations ("S" and "✱") inside small circles to represent Sea Salt and Star Dust. These are effectively codes that require the user to memorize or cross-reference with the Ingredients panel on the right. HIG 1.3 (See-and-Point) states users should not have to "memorize anything" and warns against "cryptic abbreviations or codes." The "✱" symbol for Star Dust is especially opaque — it has no obvious connection to the ingredient name.
   → **Fix**: Replace abbreviations with recognizable mini-icons or spell out short ingredient names beneath each required ingredient circle. At minimum, show the full ingredient name as a tooltip or label directly below each icon. The "✱" should become a legible symbol or the word "Star."

3. **[No Cancel or Undo for Active Brewing — HIG 1.8, 1.6]**: The brewing area shows a potion at 65% completion with no visible way to cancel, pause, or undo the operation. If the user accidentally started brewing the wrong recipe or wants to change their mind, they are trapped in a process with no escape. HIG 1.8 (Forgiveness) requires "undo available for all content-changing operations" and HIG 1.6 (User Control) states the user must always be able to "quit, save, or cancel."
   → **Fix**: Add a visible "Cancel" button adjacent to the "Brew!" button, or replace the "Brew!" button with a "Cancel Brewing" button while a brew is in progress. The cancel action should refund consumed ingredients or warn the user that ingredients will be lost.

4. **[Insufficient Contrast on Required Ingredient Labels — HIG 3.2, 8.1]**: The "v x3" and "v x1" quantity labels beneath the required ingredient icons are rendered in very small, low-contrast text (light on dark teal/green circles). These labels are critical gameplay information — they tell the user how many of each ingredient are needed — but they are nearly illegible at normal viewing distance. HIG 3.2 requires "text and thin lines have sufficient contrast to be easily visible" and "small objects use high-contrast colors."
   → **Fix**: Increase the font size of quantity labels, use white or bright text on the dark circle backgrounds, and position quantity text outside the circle where it has room to breathe. Consider a format like "Sea Salt ×3" in legible body text rather than cramming "v x3" into a tiny circle overlay.

5. **[Flat Visual Hierarchy Across Three Panels — HIG 1.10]**: The recipe list, brewing area, and ingredients panel all share nearly identical visual weight — same dark background, same rounded-corner container treatment, similar proportions. The most important area (the active brewing process) does not visually dominate the screen. A user's eye has no clear landing point. HIG 1.10 requires "clear visual hierarchy — most important elements stand out" and warns against "flat visual hierarchy — everything same prominence."
   → **Fix**: Visually elevate the center brewing panel — make it larger, use a slightly lighter or distinct background, add a subtle glow or border emphasis. The brewing area is the primary action zone and should be the clear focal point. The side panels are supportive and should feel secondary.

### Major Issues (Fix Soon)

6. **[Identical Selection Treatment for Different Element Types — HIG 1.4, 1.10]**: The selected recipe (Mana Elixir) and the active tab (Recipes) both use the same yellow/gold dashed border as their selection indicator. These are fundamentally different kinds of selection — one is a content item, the other is a navigation tab — but they are visually indistinguishable. HIG 1.4 (Consistency) requires that the same treatment communicates the same meaning, and using identical borders for different concepts creates ambiguity.
   → **Fix**: Differentiate the two selection types. Use a solid underline or filled background for the active tab, and keep the dashed border for selected list items (or vice versa). The two selection mechanisms should be visually distinct so users understand they are interacting with different types of controls.

7. **[Ingredient Icons Color-Only with Inconsistent Letter Coding — HIG 3.1, 1.4]**: The Ingredients panel uses colored circles with single letters (M for Moonpetal, F for Fire Root, S for Sea Salt, H for Honey Dew, X for Shadow Moss, ✱ for Star Dust). The letter assignments are inconsistent — "M" and "F" use the first letter of the first word, "S" could be Sea Salt or Star Dust (disambiguated by using "✱" for Star Dust), "H" uses the first letter of the first word, but "X" for Shadow Moss has no obvious connection to either word. HIG 1.4 requires consistent conventions and HIG 3.1 requires that color not be the sole differentiator.
   → **Fix**: Use a consistent abbreviation scheme (e.g., first two letters: "Mo", "Fi", "Se", "Ho", "Sh", "St") or replace letters with recognizable symbolic icons for each ingredient. The "X" for Shadow Moss and "✱" for Star Dust are particularly confusing and should be replaced with meaningful identifiers.

8. **[Progress Bar Lacks Sufficient Context — HIG 1.7]**: The brewing progress bar shows "65% Brewed" but provides no time estimate, no indication of how long the process will take, and no indication of whether the user needs to wait or can perform other actions. HIG 1.7 (Feedback and Dialog) requires "time estimates for long operations" and "clear explanation when operations can't complete."
   → **Fix**: Add an estimated time remaining (e.g., "~16s remaining") next to the percentage, or display elapsed/total time. If the user can navigate away during brewing, indicate this. If they cannot, make the modal nature of the wait explicit.

9. **[Inactive Tabs Lack Affordance — HIG 1.3, 2.7]**: The "Inventory" and "Journal" tabs have no border, no background fill, and no visual weight — they appear as plain floating text. Without the context of the highlighted "Recipes" tab next to them, a user might not recognize them as interactive elements at all. HIG 1.3 requires that "all available actions are visible" and users should be able to discover features "by scanning."
   → **Fix**: Give inactive tabs a subtle but visible container treatment — a lighter border, a slightly different background shade, or an underline — so they are clearly recognizable as clickable navigation elements. They should look interactive but visually subordinate to the active tab.

10. **[Brew Button Presented During Active Brew — HIG 1.9, 2.7]**: The "Brew!" button remains prominently displayed even while a brew is actively in progress at 65%. This creates confusion about what pressing it would do — would it start a second brew? Restart the current one? Queue another? HIG 1.9 (Perceived Stability) requires that "unavailable items are dimmed, not removed" and HIG 2.7 requires button labels to "describe the action that will occur."
    → **Fix**: While brewing is in progress, either dim/disable the "Brew!" button with a visual disabled state, or change its label to reflect the current state (e.g., "Brewing..." as a disabled label, or "Cancel" as an active alternative). The button's current state implies it is pressable, but its effect is ambiguous.

11. **[Excessive Color Palette — HIG 3.1]**: The screen uses at least 10 distinct hue-saturated colors: red (Healing Potion), blue (Mana Elixir), orange (Fire Bomb), green (Antidote), yellow/gold (selection borders, Gold counter, Brew button), teal (Sea Salt, required ingredient icons), purple/magenta (Star Dust), olive/brown (Honey Dew), dark green (Shadow Moss circle), and coral/pink (Fire Root). HIG 3.1 recommends a "limited palette (4–7 colors max for coding)" and warns against a "rainbow of colors overwhelming the interface."
    → **Fix**: Reduce the functional color palette. Group ingredients by category using 3–4 colors rather than giving each a unique hue. Reserve bright, saturated colors for interactive elements and status indicators. Muted tones should be used for decorative or secondary information.

### Minor Issues (Consider Fixing)

12. **[Monospace/Pixel Font Reduces Readability — HIG 7.1, 1.10]**: All text on the screen uses a monospace or pixel-style typeface, including potion descriptions ("Restores 50 HP"), ingredient names ("Moonpetal"), and the status bar. Monospace fonts have lower natural readability for prose and labels because uniform character widths disrupt the word shapes readers rely on for quick scanning. While thematically appropriate for a game aesthetic, it makes dense informational text (ingredient lists, potion stats) harder to parse quickly.
    → **Fix**: If the monospace font is a deliberate stylistic choice, compensate with increased font size, generous letter-spacing, and shorter label text. Consider using a proportional font for body/description text while keeping monospace for headings and decorative elements, or select a monospace font with clearly differentiated letterforms.

13. **[Potion Brew Time Not Visually Prominent — HIG 1.7, 1.10]**: Each recipe in the left panel shows a brew time (30s, 45s, 60s, 20s) in small, low-contrast text beneath the potion description. This is important decision-making information — it helps the user choose which potion to brew — but it is the least visually prominent element in each recipe card, easily overlooked. HIG 1.7 requires feedback to be "brief, direct" and clearly connected to the relevant context.
    → **Fix**: Give brew times more visual weight — use a distinct color, a small clock icon prefix, or position them on the same line as the potion name. Format consistently (e.g., "⏱ 45s") so users can scan brew times at a glance when comparing recipes.

14. **[Bottom Status Bar Low Contrast — HIG 3.2, 8.1]**: The footer text "Alchemy Level: 12 | Recipes Known: 4 | Potions Brewed: 47" is rendered in small, muted text against the dark background. While this is secondary information, it still needs to be legible. The pipe-separated format also makes it harder to scan than structured alternatives. HIG 3.2 requires sufficient contrast for text to be "easily visible."
    → **Fix**: Increase the contrast of the status bar text slightly — it doesn't need to compete with primary content, but it should be comfortably readable. Consider using a subtle background strip or divider above the status bar to visually separate it from the main content area.

15. **[Inconsistent Panel Internal Spacing — HIG 1.4]**: The three panels have noticeably different internal padding and content density. The left recipe panel has tight vertical spacing between recipe entries. The center brewing panel has generous vertical spacing with large gaps between the bottle, progress bar, ingredients, and button. The right ingredients panel uses a grid layout with moderate spacing. This inconsistency makes the layout feel unpolished and ad hoc.
    → **Fix**: Establish a consistent spacing scale (e.g., 8px, 16px, 24px increments) and apply it uniformly across all panels. Internal padding from container edge to content should be identical in all three panels. Vertical rhythm between elements should follow the same scale.

16. **[Gold Counter Isolated from Context — HIG 1.10, 1.7]**: The "Gold: 2,450" indicator sits alone in the top-right corner with a yellow border, visually disconnected from any action or decision point. The user cannot see how gold relates to brewing (Does brewing cost gold? How much? Can they afford it?). HIG 1.7 requires that information be contextually connected to the actions it supports.
    → **Fix**: If brewing costs gold, display the cost next to the "Brew!" button (e.g., "Brew! (50g)") so the user can see both their balance and the cost at the decision point. If gold is not related to brewing, consider moving it to the status bar or a dedicated economy panel to reduce visual noise in the crafting interface.

17. **[Potion Bottle Illustration Lacks Detail Relative to Screen Importance — HIG 1.10, 1.1]**: The center brewing area is the screen's primary action zone, but the potion bottle illustration is a simple, blocky silhouette with a blue rectangle for liquid. For a crafting interface themed around alchemy, the bottle metaphor (HIG 1.1) is appropriate but underdelivered — it doesn't communicate the magical, transformative nature of brewing and feels like a placeholder.
    → **Fix**: Enhance the bottle illustration with more detail — liquid color matching the potion type (blue for mana), bubbling or glow effects during brewing, a fill level that matches the progress percentage. The visual metaphor should reinforce the crafting fantasy and provide visual feedback about the brewing state.

18. **[No Visual Feedback for Ingredient Sufficiency — HIG 1.7, 3.2]**: The required ingredients section shows "S x3" and "✱ x1" but does not visually indicate whether the user has enough of each ingredient. The user must mentally cross-reference with the Ingredients panel (Sea Salt x12, Star Dust x1) to verify they can brew. HIG 1.7 requires that feedback be immediate and connected to the relevant context.
    → **Fix**: Add a visual indicator showing ingredient sufficiency — green check for "have enough," red X for "insufficient," or show the format "3/12" (needed/owned). This removes the need for mental cross-referencing and makes the brewing decision immediate.

### Strengths

- **Strong Thematic Metaphor**: The "Alchemist's Workshop" theme is a clear, engaging real-world metaphor that users immediately understand. The alchemy/potion-crafting concept maps well to a crafting interface with recipes, ingredients, and a brewing process (HIG 1.1).
- **Logical Three-Panel Layout**: The screen is organized into a sensible workflow — browse recipes (left), execute brewing (center), check inventory (right). This left-to-right flow matches natural reading order and creates a coherent mental model of the crafting process.
- **Clear Tab Navigation**: The top-level tabs (Recipes, Inventory, Journal) provide clear, discoverable navigation between major sections of the workshop. The active tab is visually distinguished from inactive ones.
- **Progress Bar Provides Brewing Feedback**: The 65% progress bar with percentage label gives users a clear sense of how far along the brewing process is, satisfying HIG 1.7's requirement for progress indicators during operations.
- **Consistent Dark Theme**: The overall dark background with lighter panel containers creates a cohesive visual identity appropriate for a game UI. The color scheme is atmospheric and thematically fitting for an alchemy workshop.
- **Recipe List Shows Key Decision Info**: Each recipe entry displays the potion name, effect description, and brew time — the three pieces of information a user needs to choose which potion to brew.

### Overall Score: 4/10

The Alchemist's Workshop demonstrates solid structural thinking — the three-panel layout, tab navigation, and recipe-to-brewing workflow are logically sound and create a coherent user experience. However, the screen suffers from significant accessibility and usability failures. Color is the sole differentiator for both potion types and ingredient icons, violating one of the HIG's most fundamental principles. Cryptic single-letter abbreviations force users to memorize or cross-reference rather than recognize at a glance. The active brewing state offers no cancel option and presents an ambiguous "Brew!" button that appears actionable during an in-progress operation. The visual hierarchy is flat across the three panels, making the primary action zone (brewing) no more prominent than the supporting panels. The excessive color palette (10+ distinct hues) overwhelms rather than organizes, and critical information like ingredient sufficiency requires mental arithmetic rather than immediate visual feedback. While the thematic metaphor is strong and the layout bones are sound, the execution needs significant refinement to meet basic usability and accessibility standards.
