# Design Audit: Simple Button Screen

*Audited against the Apple Human Interface Guidelines (1987 Desktop Interface)*
*Date: February 7, 2026*

---

## Audit Summary: Simple Button Demo

### Critical Issues (Fix Immediately)

1. **[Button Labels Describe Style, Not Action — HIG 2.7]**: Every button label on screen describes the button's visual variant ("Secondary," "Outline," "Small," "Pill," "Buttons") rather than the action it would perform. HIG 2.7 explicitly requires that button "labels describe the action that will occur." The primary button's label "Click Me!" is equally uninformative — it tells the user to click but not what clicking will accomplish. In a showcase intended as a reference for developers, this teaches the wrong pattern: labeling buttons by appearance rather than by purpose.
   → **Fix**: Use realistic action verbs for button labels — at minimum for one row. For example, replace "Secondary" with "Save," "Outline" with "Cancel." If the intent is to showcase visual styles, add a subtitle or tooltip identifying the style while keeping the label as a verb. The primary button could read "Submit" or "Confirm" to demonstrate proper labeling.

2. **[Color as Sole Differentiator Between Button Variants — HIG 3.1, 8.1]**: The five lower buttons (Secondary, Outline, Small, Pill, Buttons) are distinguished almost entirely by fill color — mint green, white, mint, pink, and golden-yellow. On a monochrome display or for a colorblind user, the mint "Secondary" and mint "Small" would appear nearly identical. HIG 3.1 states that "color coding is redundant with shape/position/pattern" and that the design must "work in black and white first."
   → **Fix**: Add a secondary visual differentiator for each variant: different border weight, different icon, different text weight, or a pattern/texture within the fill. Each button variant must be distinguishable without color. At minimum, ensure no two buttons share the same hue without a clear structural difference.

3. **[No Default Button Indicated — HIG 2.7]**: The large "Click Me!" button appears to be the primary action, but it does not use the HIG-standard double-outline treatment to indicate it is the default. HIG 2.7 requires that a "default button [is] doubly outlined" to show which action Return/Enter would activate. Without this, keyboard users have no way to identify the primary action at a glance.
   → **Fix**: Apply a double-outline or equivalent prominent visual treatment to the primary button to clearly mark it as the default. This should be distinct from the decorative drop-shadow border currently applied. Modern equivalents include a heavier border weight or a distinct "primary" fill treatment that is unambiguously stronger than other buttons.

4. **[Insufficient Contrast on Subtitle Text — HIG 3.2, 8.1]**: The subtitle "Go ahead, give it a click!" is rendered in a very light gray handwritten font against the white card background. The contrast ratio appears well below the 4.5:1 minimum needed for readable body text. Similarly, the "More Styles" section label is extremely small and faint. HIG 3.2 requires that "text and thin lines have sufficient contrast to be easily visible," and HIG 8.1 requires high contrast availability for vision accessibility.
   → **Fix**: Darken the subtitle text to at least a medium gray (#666 or darker) and increase the font size of "More Styles" so it is legible at normal viewing distances. All instructional or label text should meet WCAG AA contrast requirements (4.5:1 for normal text, 3:1 for large text).

5. **[Handwritten/Decorative Typography Reduces Legibility — HIG 7.1, 1.10]**: The entire card uses a handwritten/cursive-style typeface for the title, subtitle, button labels, and status text. While charming aesthetically, cursive fonts have significantly lower legibility than standard sans-serif fonts — letter shapes are less distinct, word recognition is slower, and characters can be ambiguous (particularly at small sizes). HIG 7.1 requires "concise and simple" communication, and HIG 1.10 (Aesthetic Integrity) warns that "graphics support understanding, not just decoration."
   → **Fix**: Reserve the handwritten font for the title/heading only, where it contributes personality. Use a clear, well-kerned sans-serif or proportional font for button labels, status text, and section labels where instant readability is critical. If the handwritten font is a deliberate game-UI design decision, increase font size and letter spacing to compensate for reduced legibility.

### Major Issues (Fix Soon)

6. **[Flat Visual Hierarchy Among Button Variants — HIG 1.10]**: The five lower buttons ("Secondary," "Outline," "Small," "Pill," "Buttons") all compete at roughly the same visual weight. There is no clear indication of which is primary, which is secondary, or which is tertiary. The "Secondary" and "Outline" buttons are visually comparable in size and prominence, while the bottom row of three smaller buttons reads as a separate group but with no clear hierarchy among them. HIG 1.10 requires "clear visual hierarchy — most important elements stand out."
   → **Fix**: Establish a clear three-tier visual hierarchy: primary (largest, boldest fill), secondary (medium, lighter treatment), and tertiary (smallest, most subtle). Use size, fill weight, and border treatment together to create unmistakable levels of importance. Group related buttons and separate groups with more whitespace or dividers.

7. **[Decorative Background Elements Distract from Content — HIG 1.10]**: The outer background contains scattered pastel dots (pink, mint, blue, peach, yellow) at various positions. These serve no functional purpose and draw the eye away from the interactive content in the card. HIG 1.10 warns against "decoration that distracts from function" and states that graphics should "support understanding."
   → **Fix**: Remove or significantly reduce the decorative dot elements. If they serve a branding purpose, confine them to a border region or reduce their opacity/size so they do not compete with the interactive content. The card itself should be the clear focal point.

8. **[No Visible Feedback or Interactive States — HIG 1.7, 5.1]**: The showcase displays only the resting/default state of each button. No hover, focus, pressed, or disabled states are shown or demonstrated. HIG 1.7 requires "immediate feedback confirms every action" and HIG 5.1 requires "visual feedback shows what's selected." A button showcase that omits interactive states is incomplete as a reference implementation.
   → **Fix**: Add visual demonstrations of interactive states — either as additional button rows (Default, Hover, Pressed, Disabled, Focused) or as a live interactive area where users can see state changes in real time. At minimum, show a disabled variant to demonstrate how unavailability is communicated.

9. **[Status Text "No clicks yet..." Lacks Clear Purpose — HIG 1.7]**: The status label "No clicks yet..." is displayed in a rounded gray badge but does not clearly explain what it is counting, which button it relates to, or what will change when a click occurs. HIG 1.7 requires feedback that is "brief, direct, uses user vocabulary." The ellipsis and passive phrasing ("No clicks yet...") feels vague rather than informative.
   → **Fix**: Make the status text explicit: "Click count: 0" or "You haven't clicked the button yet." Associate it visually with the specific button it tracks (e.g., place it directly below the "Click Me!" button with a connecting line or shared container). Remove the trailing ellipsis, which implies something is loading or incomplete.

10. **[Inconsistent Border Treatments Across Button Variants — HIG 1.4]**: The "Click Me!" button has a thick dark border with a pronounced offset shadow/bevel. "Secondary" has a thin colored border. "Outline" has a thin dark border with no fill. The small buttons have thin colored borders matching their pastel fills. At least four distinct border treatments are visible for elements that are all fundamentally the same component type (button). HIG 1.4 (Consistency) requires that "visual styles [are] consistent throughout."
   → **Fix**: Establish a consistent border system: one border weight for standard buttons, one for outline variants, and one for the primary/default button. The current mix of thick bevel borders, thin color-matched borders, and thin dark borders reads as accidental inconsistency rather than intentional variation.

11. **[Small Button Hit Targets — HIG 5.2, 8.3]**: The bottom row of buttons ("Small," "Pill," "Buttons") appears to have very small click/touch target areas. If these represent a "small" size variant, they risk falling below the minimum recommended touch target size (44x44pt per Apple's modern guidelines, and the general usability principle that controls must be easy to click). HIG 8.3 notes that interfaces should be accessible to users with motor impairments.
   → **Fix**: Ensure all interactive buttons have a minimum hit target of 44x44 points, even if the visual footprint is smaller (use invisible padding to extend the tappable area). If "Small" is a size variant, document that it is intended for dense desktop UIs only and not recommended for touch interfaces.

### Minor Issues (Consider Fixing)

12. **[Exclamation Mark in "Click Me!" Creates Unnecessary Urgency — HIG 7.1]**: The exclamation mark on "Click Me!" adds a tone of urgency or excitement that is not warranted by the button's actual function. HIG 7.1 advises text that is "friendly without being misleading." In a UI component showcase, overly enthusiastic punctuation models poor label design for developers who reference the toolkit.
    → **Fix**: Remove the exclamation mark. "Click Me" or better yet a descriptive verb label ("Submit," "Confirm") is sufficient. Button labels should be calm, clear, and descriptive.

13. **[Card Has No Navigation or Contextual Chrome — HIG 2.1, 1.9]**: The card floats on the decorative background with no title bar, close button, back arrow, or any navigation element. Users cannot tell how to leave this screen, return to a gallery, or access other demos. HIG 2.1 requires standard window components and HIG 1.9 (Perceived Stability) requires "familiar landmarks" in the interface.
    → **Fix**: Add minimal navigation chrome — at least a back arrow or breadcrumb ("Gallery > Simple Button") at the top of the card, and ensure the card has a clear relationship to the broader showcase structure.

14. **[Section Label "More Styles" Is Too Small and Lacks Visual Weight — HIG 1.10]**: The "More Styles" label that separates the primary button from the variant buttons is rendered in an extremely small, light font. It barely registers as a section divider. HIG 1.10 requires that "different things look different" — a section heading should be visually distinguishable from body text and button labels.
    → **Fix**: Increase the font size and weight of "More Styles" or replace it with a clear horizontal divider with a label. Use a visual treatment that unambiguously reads as a section separator — heavier font weight, uppercase lettering, or a horizontal rule.

15. **[Pastel Color Palette May Fail in Varied Lighting — HIG 3.1, 8.1]**: The entire screen uses extremely low-saturation pastel colors (light coral, pale mint, soft pink, muted yellow) against a cream/off-white background. In bright ambient lighting or on low-quality displays, these subtle color differences may wash out entirely, making buttons hard to distinguish from the background. HIG 3.1 warns against "subtle color differences for important distinctions."
    → **Fix**: Increase the saturation of button fill colors so they remain distinguishable in varied viewing conditions. Alternatively, add stronger border contrast so buttons remain clearly defined even when fill colors wash out. Test the palette under high-brightness conditions and on low-contrast displays.

16. **[No Cancel or Escape Route — HIG 1.8]**: The demo presents interactive buttons but offers no visible way to undo or cancel an action. If "Click Me!" triggers a state change (incrementing a counter), there is no reset or undo mechanism shown. HIG 1.8 (Forgiveness) requires that "users can explore without fear of breaking things" and that "undo [is] available for all content-changing operations."
    → **Fix**: Add a visible "Reset" button near the click counter, or demonstrate an undo mechanism. Even for a simple demo, showing that actions are reversible teaches good interaction patterns.

17. **[Inconsistent Corner Radius Values — HIG 1.4]**: The card container uses a large corner radius (approximately 16–20px). The "Click Me!" button uses a medium radius (approximately 8–12px). The "Secondary" button appears to use a similar radius, but the "Outline" button appears slightly different. The small buttons vary as well — "Pill" appears to use a fully rounded/capsule shape while "Small" and "Buttons" use a standard radius. Multiple corner radius values for the same component type creates visual inconsistency.
    → **Fix**: Define two or three standard corner radius tokens (e.g., `radius-sm: 6px`, `radius-md: 10px`, `radius-full: 9999px`) and assign each button variant to exactly one. The "Pill" variant should be explicitly identified as using `radius-full` while all others use `radius-md`.

18. **[White Text on Coral Background May Fail Contrast — HIG 3.2]**: The "Click Me!" button uses white text on a coral/salmon (#F07070-ish) background. Depending on the exact values, this may fall below the 3:1 minimum contrast ratio required for large text. The handwritten font's thin strokes exacerbate this — thin letterforms require even more contrast to remain legible than bold typefaces. HIG 3.2 specifically flags "low-contrast text or thin lines" as a red flag.
    → **Fix**: Darken the coral button fill slightly (e.g., to #D05050) or use a heavier font weight for the label. Verify the actual contrast ratio with a tool and ensure it meets at least 3:1 for the rendered text size, ideally 4.5:1.

### Strengths

- **Clear Card-Based Layout**: The centered card on a distinct background creates an obvious content boundary and focal point, making it immediately clear where the interactive content lives.
- **Logical Content Grouping**: The primary button is prominently placed at top, the status indicator sits directly below it, and variant buttons are organized into a separate "More Styles" section — the information architecture flows logically from most important to supplementary.
- **Friendly, Approachable Tone**: The subtitle "Go ahead, give it a click!" and the status "No clicks yet..." create a welcoming, low-pressure invitation to interact, which is appropriate for a demo/showcase context.
- **Interactive Feedback Mechanism**: The click counter ("No clicks yet...") demonstrates that buttons produce observable results, supporting HIG 1.7's requirement for feedback — the concept is sound even if the execution needs refinement.
- **Warm, Cohesive Color Palette**: Despite accessibility concerns, the pastel palette is internally cohesive — the coral primary, mint secondary, pink accent, and golden tertiary work harmoniously together, creating a unified aesthetic identity.

### Overall Score: 3/10

The Simple Button Demo has a warm, approachable aesthetic and sound information architecture — the card layout, content grouping, and friendly tone demonstrate thoughtful design intent. However, it suffers from fundamental usability and accessibility failures that undermine its purpose as a component showcase. Button labels describe visual styles rather than actions, violating the most basic button labeling convention. Color is the sole differentiator between variants, failing colorblind users entirely. The decorative handwritten typography, while charming, significantly reduces legibility for labels and status text where clarity is paramount. Contrast failures on subtitle text and potentially on the primary button's white-on-coral treatment would make the screen partially unreadable for users with low vision. For a toolkit reference implementation, these issues are especially concerning: developers who reference this demo will inherit its bad patterns. The visual craft is appealing, but the interaction design and accessibility fundamentals need substantial rework before this screen can serve as a reliable guide for building real UI.
